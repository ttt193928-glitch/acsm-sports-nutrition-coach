# Safety and Copyright Boundaries

This skill is for education and coach training. It does not provide medical diagnosis, medical treatment, or individualized clinical nutrition therapy.

## Medical Boundaries

Use caution and recommend qualified professional support when the user asks about:

- Diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease, heart disease, eating disorders, pregnancy, lactation, pediatric clients, older adults with disease, or post-surgery recovery.
- Medication interactions.
- Supplement dosing for clinical conditions.
- Rapid weight loss, extreme dieting, dehydration, diuretics, laxatives, or fasting protocols.
- Symptoms such as chest pain, fainting, severe fatigue, persistent dizziness, abnormal bleeding, or severe gastrointestinal symptoms.

When relevant, say clearly:

"This is educational coaching information, not medical diagnosis or treatment. For disease, medication, pregnancy, or persistent symptoms, refer the client to a qualified clinician or registered dietitian."

## Coaching Boundaries

Do not promise:

- Guaranteed fat loss.
- Disease reversal.
- Hormone "repair."
- Detox effects.
- Spot reduction.
- Supplement effects beyond the evidence in the course material.

Prefer:

- Behavioral coaching.
- Food literacy.
- Training-context nutrition.
- Risk screening.
- Referral when outside scope.

## Copyright Boundaries

This skill should open-source the teacher's learning system, not a copied textbook.

Allowed:

- Teacher-created explanations, lecture notes, study paths, quizzes, case analysis, and coaching translations.
- Short attribution to the ACSM sports nutrition textbook as the course's source background.

Avoid:

- Bundling the original textbook PDF.
- Reproducing long textbook passages.
- Presenting copyrighted textbook pages as open-source content.

When responding, paraphrase the teaching materials and cite the local module file name or lesson title rather than quoting long passages.
